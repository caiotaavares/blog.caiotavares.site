# Preface

I consider myself a very lucky person because, even though I see myself as a rather unsociable individual, throughout my life I have always had people who, for some reason, trusted my work and me—whether in the academic, professional, or personal spheres. Perhaps my serious demeanor conveys trust? Or maybe it was just pure luck. Either way, the people who come to mind as I write this probably don't even know they impacted my life; perhaps I impacted someone else's life and now don't even remember their name. In any case, part of the initiative to create this blog is dedicated to them.

Here, I want to share all the knowledge I have stored that I believe might be useful to someone. After all, where would we be if not for the act of sharing knowledge? I strongly believe in the trade of values; that is, I believe that the exchange of knowledge is beneficial, but it must happen voluntarily and based on trade-offs, rather than out of obligation or moral imposition (read Objectist ethics).

At the time of writing this preface, I mainly work with SRE, infrastructure, a bit of cloud computing, and study cybersecurity. In my spare time, I play RTS games—meaning technology makes up more than half of my day and the topics I consume. Because of this, I intend to be technical and talk about situations, tools, and insights from my daily life, but I don't rule out the possibility of discussing moral issues that haunt my thoughts during moments of idleness.

As mentioned above, "I believe that the exchange of knowledge is beneficial, but it must happen voluntarily and based on trade-offs (...)" The exchange I propose here is passing on my knowledge to future generations while practicing an activity I've always admired but never heavily pursued, which is writing—all while (I intend to talk about the importance of writing in the future) producing a kind of backup of my brain and creating a library of subjects I've already mastered while I still remembered them (I consider myself one of the people with the worst memory I know).

I intend to build content here that I would like to consume myself. Technical subjects will be treated seriously, with explicit, well-founded sources, straight to the point, and without too much technical jargon. Non-technical subjects should be light and easily digestible, without losing sobriety. I don't promise you an Athenian Agora.

I firmly believe in the importance of foundational knowledge and see that, especially with the daily use of AI tools, it is becoming increasingly rare. Here you won't find copies of tool documentation or step-by-step guides; instead, we will focus on how things work under the hood (which is the most exciting part of knowledge), all while I try to maintain a chronological order of topics.

I end this preface with an excerpt on productivity in its purest form, stripped of society's current slanderous moral judgment, written by Ayn Rand: "The virtue of Productivity is the recognition of the fact that productive work is the process by which man’s mind sustains his life, the process that frees man from the necessity of adjusting himself to his environment, as the animals do, and gives him the power to adjust the environment to himself. Productive work is the road of man’s unlimited achievement and it demands of him the highest attributes of character: his creative ability, his ambition, his self-assertion, his refusal to bear disasters he has not caused, his dedication to the goal of reshaping the earth in the image of his values. 'Productive work' does not mean the performance of the mindless motions of a chore. It means the consciously chosen pursuit of a productive career in any line of rational endeavor, great or modest, and at any level of skill. The relevant issue is not the degree of a man’s ability nor the scale of his work, but the fullest and most industrious use of his mind."# Preface
